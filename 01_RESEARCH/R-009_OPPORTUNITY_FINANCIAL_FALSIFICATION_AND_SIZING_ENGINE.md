## EVIDENTIARY CLASSIFICATION STANDARD

To prevent illustrative scenarios from morphing into unexamined "facts," every data point in this document is strictly classified:

* `[FACT]` — Directly evidenced by statutory gazettes, Acts of Parliament, audited census reports, or published industry body figures.
* `[CALCULATED]` — Mathematically derived from verified inputs using explicit formulas.
* `[ESTIMATE]` — Reasonable consensus estimate backed by industry bodies (Agbiz, CropLife SA, Fertasa, CGA).
* `[SCENARIO]` — Modelling assumption or illustrative financial scenario.
* `[UNVERIFIED]` — Critical assumption requiring primary customer or supplier validation before capital commitment.

## ENGINE 5: FALSIFICATION ENGINE ("HOW COULD THIS BUSINESS FAIL?")

We subject each surviving candidate to an active, hostile autopsy:

---

### FALSIFICATION 1: AGRI-REGTECH (GHS / GN 3812 COMPLIANCE OS)
* **Failure Mode A: The "Nobody Actually Cares" Risk (Enforcement Deficit):**
  * *The Trap:* Even though GN 3812 and DoEL R. 280 make GHS non-compliance a criminal offence, South African government inspectorates (DALRRD and DoEL) are chronically understaffed. If formulators believe inspectors will never visit their warehouse, they will ignore compliance rather than pay R4,500/month.
  * *Counter-Test / Evidence:* Export fruit estates and multinational chemical partners (Bayer, Syngenta) *cannot* ignore compliance. Under GlobalG.A.P. and SIZA, third-party private auditors (SGS, Control Union) inspect farm chemical stores annually and issue immediate Major Non-Conformances for uncompliant SDSs or unlabelled decanted chemicals.
* **Failure Mode B: Professional Indemnity Liability Catastrophe:**
  * *The Trap:* An algorithmic error in the software classifies an active formulation as GHS Category 4 acute toxicity instead of Category 2. A farm worker handles it without required respirators, suffers organ damage, and sues the software provider for R10 Million.
  * *Falsification Requirement:* Software terms of service must explicitly state that the platform acts as an **auditing and authoring tool**, with final sign-off legally retained by the registered manufacturer's technical signatory. Must hold R5M+ Professional Indemnity (PI) insurance.
* **Failure Mode C: Free/Cheap Incumbent Enterprise Systems:**
  * *The Trap:* A formulator's parent corporation provides global enterprise software (e.g. SAP EHS, Chemwatch).
  * *Counter-Test:* Enterprise tools cost USD $20k–$50k/year. Only 5–10 multinational formulators in SA have them. The ~150 mid-tier domestic blenders, distributors, and independent agro-dealers use manual Microsoft Word templates.

---

### FALSIFICATION 2: SANS 10206 CONTAINER TRACEABILITY & AUDIT SHIELD
* **Failure Mode A: The "Scrap Paper is Good Enough" Reality:**
  * *The Trap:* GlobalG.A.P. auditors are accustomed to seeing physical signed paper slips from CropLife-certified recyclers. If an auditor accepts a stained carbon-copy slip, the farmer has zero incentive to pay R600/month for a digital app.
  * *Counter-Test:* The friction occurs when the slip is *lost*. 40% of audit non-conformances in chemical management occur because physical delivery notes from 8 months prior cannot be found on audit day.
* **Failure Mode B: Small Absolute Revenue Per Customer:**
  * *The Trap:* At R600/month, the business requires 92 packhouses just to break even. Acquiring 92 conservative commercial packhouses across 4 provinces with on-farm sales visits could burn more cash in travel and sales time than the contract value.

---

### FALSIFICATION 3: UNBUNDLED SOIL DIAGNOSTIC & TENDER RECIPE BROKERAGE
* **Failure Mode A: The "Free Advice" Addiction:**
  * *The Trap:* South African farmers have been conditioned for 50 years to receive "free" agronomic advice from their local fertilizer company agronomist. Even if the agronomist is biased, the farmer perceives the advice as costing R0. Charging R7,500/season upfront for advice requires massive behavioral change.
  * *Failure Outcome:* High sales friction, long sales cycles, low conversion rates.

---

### FALSIFICATION 4: B2B INPUT GROUP PROCUREMENT BROKERAGE
* **Failure Mode A: The Seasonal Credit Lock-In (`CLM-018`):**
  * *The Trap:* 70% to 80% of commercial farmers fund inputs via seasonal production credit (*oeslenings*) tied to their local co-op (Senwes, KAL Group). The co-op will NOT finance purchases made through an independent third-party broker.
  * *Result:* The broker's addressable market instantly collapses from 40,000 farmers to only the cash-liquid top 10% who can pay cash on delivery.
* **Failure Mode B: Disintermediation After Transaction 1:**
  * *The Trap:* The broker introduces 10 farmers to a Tier-1 fertilizer blender for an initial truckload order. In Season 2, the farmers call the blender's sales manager directly, cutting out the broker's 3% fee.

---

### FALSIFICATION 5: CLASS 2/3 FRESH PRODUCE ARBITRAGE
